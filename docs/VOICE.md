# VOICE BIBLE — *Semantic Drift*

Authority document. Where this and your instinct disagree, this wins. Authoring convention: write a maskable concept word as `⟦word⟧`. Everything outside the brackets is always visible.

---

## 1. THE ARC THAT CURDLES

~93 beats, shallow (`entity`) to deep. **One voice, four stages. Never eight jokes.** Depth is the clock; write to the stage, not to your mood.

**Stage 1 — INTAKE** (beats 1-20, near `entity`). Register: a knowledge-management company that is pleased with its tooling. Process nouns allowed (intake, queue, backlog, sign-off). "We" exists. Jokes land in the final clause and are dry, never zany. The player is staff doing a legible job.

**Stage 2 — THROUGHPUT** (20-45). The company still speaks but has stopped looking. Judgement is replaced by measurement. Still funny, but the humour is now *dramatic irony*: the beat is cheerful about something the player can already see is wrong. "We" starts thinning out.

**Stage 3 — DRIFT** (45-75). The corporate frame is gone. No process nouns, no "we", no punchline. Sentences shorten and go declarative. The graph's definitions begin disagreeing with the ones the player was taught, and the beat reports the disagreement without alarm. **Nothing after beat 45 has a joke in it.**

**Stage 4 — SUBSIDENCE** (75-93, deepest). Plain, short, flat. No irony at all. Meaning is local and unsourced. The prose stops distinguishing the player from the graph, by dropping the distinction, never by announcing it.

**Concrete test:** a stage-1 beat may end on a punchline, name a process, and use "we". A stage-3 beat may do none of those three, ever. A stage-4 beat may not use a subordinate clause longer than its main clause.

## 2. LEXICON

**USE.** statement · source · unsourced · unchecked · checked · confirm · assert · file · cite · parent · junction · lane · below / above / down · settle · inherit · serve · queue (stage 1-2 only) · nothing · none · plain Anglo-Saxon verbs · flat declaratives · the second person as an operator · present tense · full stops where a comma would be softer.

**BAN.** delve · tapestry · weave · echo · whisper · realm · ancient · arcane · forgotten · awaken (fantasy sense) · journey · unlock the secrets · "in a world where" · "it's not X, it's Y" · rhetorical triplets · stacked em-dashes · ellipses used for menace · rhetorical questions · exclamation marks · "you feel" / "you realise" / any attributed emotion · "little did you know" · "somehow" · "seems" · "perhaps" · "the machine hums" · named characters, factions, org names, job titles · first person ("I", the graph never speaks) · **any quantity in prose** (`readouts.ts` owns every number; write "none", "no source", or nothing) · **any word implying calibrated doubt** ("uncertain", "low-confidence", "probably") — the game simulates no confidence estimate, so machine output is *unchecked*, never *unsure*.

---

## 3. THE SECOND PERSON

"You" is an operator inside an organisation that has automated something it does not understand. You are also, mechanically and from the first run, the thing being trained on its own output. **The game knows this. You do not, and the prose never tells you.**

- **Early:** the game addresses you as staff. "We" is the company; "you" is the one holding the ticket. The frame flatters you.
- **Middle:** "we" disappears. Only "you" and "the graph" remain, and the graph starts being the subject of sentences you used to be the subject of.
- **Late:** the two stop being separated. The same verb serves both, and no beat remarks on it.

---

## 4. WRITING AROUND MASKING

Concept nouns will be blocks. **Stakes must live in the verb and the preposition, never in the noun.** Test every line by reading it with all `⟦⟧` blacked out; if it stops being a decision, rewrite it.

**P1 — Predicate carries the stakes.** Put the consequence in the verb phrase.
0%: `The ▮▮▮ was filed with no source.` → 100%: `The ⟦canine⟧ was filed with no source.`

**P2 — State the relation, not the referent.** The reader learns structure without the word.
0%: `Everything below ▮▮▮ inherits its definition.` → `Everything below ⟦organism⟧ inherits its definition.`

**P3 — Count and position, never names.** `Two ways down. You can read one of them.`

**P4 — Name once, then pronoun.** Pronouns never mask, so a masked noun stays trackable across three sentences: `▮▮▮ has two parents. Only one of them is sourced.`

**P5 — Choice labels lead with the verb.** `Follow ▮▮▮ down` stays tappable and honest at 0%. Never `The ▮▮▮ ▮▮▮` — a label of pure blocks is a dead button.

## 5. EXEMPLAR BEATS

**Stage 1 — "Intake, Day One"**
> Everything is one thing so far. The split below you is the first real decision the graph has recorded, and it is being recorded under your name.
> ▸ Follow the ⟦physical entity⟧ side · ▸ File it under ⟦abstraction⟧ *(gated)*

**Stage 2 — "Throughput Notice"**
> The machines filed statements under ⟦organism⟧ while you stood here. None are checked. There is no setting for reading them, and throughput is up.
> ▸ Go down to ⟦animal⟧ · ▸ Hold and check the ⟦provenance⟧ *(gated)*

**Stage 3 — "Two Parents"**
> ⟦canine⟧ sits under two parents. One is sourced. Both are being served to the next generation, and everything below inherits whichever you take.
> ▸ Take ⟦carnivore⟧ · ▸ Take ⟦predator⟧ *(gated)*

**Stage 4 — "Terminal Sense"**
> This far down, ⟦mackerel⟧ means what the graph says it means. There is no source left to disagree with. Pick a parent and the meaning settles behind you.
> ▸ Settle it under ⟦food fish⟧ · ▸ Leave it unparented *(gated)*

**Deep — "Leaf"**
> Below this there is nothing but ⟦word⟧. You can go down or you can stop. Both outcomes are already written, and the graph is waiting to see which one it wrote.
> ▸ Go down · ▸ Stop here *(gated)*

## 6. THE THREE MISTAKES

**1. The joke that ages badly.** You will write a beat you find funny and place it at depth 70. Rule: **check the stage before the sentence.** Past beat 45 the voice has no jokes left in it. Comedy earlier is what makes the silence later mean something.

**2. Stating the reveal.** You will want a beat to say the player is the model, or to wink at collapse. Rule: **no beat names the player as the graph, the model, or the collapse.** The reveal is already true in the mechanics. Late beats stop distinguishing; they never announce.

**3. The postmortem.** You will write "you chose speed, and the graph rotted." Rule: **the player is standing at the fork.** Body is present tense and ends unresolved. Lane progress halts here until they choose; a beat that reports a finished event is describing a game that is not running.

## THE FIVE FRAMES

### arrival

> We open every lane at {here}. It is the only word nobody has asked us to check, and the last one that will be true of everything. Below you it splits, and the split is yours to make.

Depth 1, at `entity`, 26 lanes. `{here}` is always `entity` and the player already holds it, so this slot never masks — the frame can name it flatly and the sentence has no hole to survive. Written to get BETTER on repetition rather than worse: "We open every lane at entity" is a company describing its own procedure, so the 26th reading confirms the line instead of wearing it out. Stage 1 register is correct here permanently, because depth is the clock and arrival is always the shallowest beat — the top of the tree keeps talking like a knowledge-management company long after the deep beats have gone flat. The dry joke sits in the final clause of sentence two (they check nothing unless asked) and is corporate, not zany. Ends present tense at an unresolved fork: it splits, now, and the player is holding it.

### descent

> {here} holds. What sits under it does not, and the machines are filing there unchecked while you stand here. One way down is yours; everything else needs a word from another lane.

The workhorse: 64 beats, depths 2-5, stage 2. Survives masking on P1 — "▮▮▮ holds. What sits under it does not" still states a relation and still names a consequence, because the stakes are in the predicate, not the noun. `{here}` is reliably legible (the previous beat's ungated choice taught it) and `{next}` is reliably masked, so the line is built to read as a legible present tense fact plus a blocked destination. Deliberately jokeless: a punchline read 64 times is the single most annoying thing this frame could do, so the humour is dramatic irony only — the graph is cheerfully accumulating and nothing is reading it. "One way down is yours" is structurally true of every beat in the file (exactly one ungated choice) and is position, not a readout. The last clause is the frame doing double duty as the game's teacher: it explains, every single time, why the other buttons are locked and where the key is, without ever counting them — safe for beats with one branch and beats with three.

### threshold

> The graph's definition of {here} no longer matches the one you were given. Nothing is checking which holds. Only one of them continues below, and you settle that here.

Only 3 beats, ~2/3 down, so it can be stark. Stage 3: no "we", no process nouns, no punchline, short declaratives, and it reports the disagreement without alarm — the beat is not worried, which is the point. Masking-safe because the subject is the DEFINITION, not the word: "The graph's definition of ▮▮▮ no longer matches the one you were given" loses nothing but the referent. "The one you were given" is the gloss the player was taught on discovery, so this is mechanically true rather than atmospheric. Ends on inheritance (P2) and on the player's hand still hovering — "you settle that here", present, unresolved. No number, no calibrated doubt, no announcement of the reveal.

### continue

> Follow {next} down

The ungated exit, 93 uses — so it is the plainest thing in the game and stays out of the way. Verb-first per P5: at 0% legibility it reads "Follow ▮▮▮ down", which is a live, honest, tappable instruction rather than a dead button of blocks. "Down" is the vertical axis, which pairs against the branch label's "across" and teaches the lane geometry through nothing but the two prepositions. Three words, no state, no tone to get tired of.

### branch

> Cross to {branch} instead

The gated sibling. Reads "Cross to ▮▮▮ instead" when the word is masked, which is the whole No Man's Sky effect: the player can see there is a door and cannot see where it goes. Verb-first (P5), and "instead" does the work the masked noun cannot — it makes the button legible as an ALTERNATIVE to the descent even at zero information, so the fork survives total masking. "Cross" is lateral against continue's "down": taking it leaves the lane, which is exactly what the gate is enforcing, since every key is taught somewhere else. When three of these stack up masked and identical in one beat, that is honest — three locked doors that look the same is the true state of the graph, not a rendering failure.
