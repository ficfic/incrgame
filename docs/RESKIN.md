# Five places, reskinned — the evidence for the keep-or-rewrite call

The owner, asked whether the 37 hand-written places survive the road-crew
premise: **"show me the reskin first."** Right answer — it is a decision about
prose, and prose is judged by reading it, not by arguing about it.

Five, chosen to span the range: two that need almost nothing, one that needs a
sentence, and two that are the hardest cases in the file because they are pure
**counting spine** — the tally, the lead strip, the machine keeping a count.

The rule followed: **plain declarative sentences, nothing rhymes, nothing is
wise, the prose observes and stops.** Same register as the originals, because
the originals set the bar.

---

## 0 · The Cut — *the opening screen, so it carries the most weight*

**Now**
> A road that was a river, or a river that gave up. Water still runs somewhere
> under the stones — you can hear it arguing with itself. Downhill the cut
> widens. Uphill it does not. Someone has stacked three flat rocks at the fork,
> recently, and stacking rocks is not something the weather does.

**Reskinned**
> A road that was a river, or a river that gave up. Water still runs somewhere
> under the stones — you can hear it arguing with itself. Downhill the cut
> widens. Uphill it does not. The alignment starts at the fork: an iron stake
> driven to the collar, with a number cut into the head, and the number is one.

**Changed:** the last sentence. The stacked rocks become the first stake.
**What you lose:** *"stacking rocks is not something the weather does"* — a good
line. **What you gain:** the plan is physically present in the first thing you
read, and the game's first noun is a survey stake.

---

## 1 · The Weir — *the near-zero case*

**Now**
> The water reappears here and is immediately put to work. A wooden weir, far
> better built than anything else in the valley, holds it back for no reason you
> can see — there is no mill, no field, no house. Just a very good gate, keeping
> water from going where water was going anyway.

**Reskinned**
> The water reappears here and is immediately put to work. A wooden weir, far
> better built than anything else on the line, holds it back for no reason you
> can see — there is no mill, no field, no house. Just a very good gate, keeping
> water from going where water was going anyway. The sheet calls it *existing
> works*.

**Changed:** one word (*valley* → *line*) and one added sentence.
★ **This is the argument for keeping.** The body already says: excellent
infrastructure, no purpose, nobody here. That was atmosphere; under the new
premise it is **evidence**. And *existing works* is what an engineer's drawing
actually calls a thing that is already there — so the plan knowing about the
weir is stated in three words, in the register of the fiction.

## 109 · The Blockyard — *three words*

**Now**
> Finished blocks, dressed and stacked in rows, waiting for a building nobody
> put up. Each carries a number cut into the face. They run into the hundreds,
> in order, and the order has gaps — blocks taken from the middle, which is the
> last place a person takes one from. Below the yard the tailrace comes out of
> the hill.

**Reskinned**
> Finished blocks, dressed and stacked in rows, waiting for **a road nobody
> laid**. Each carries a number cut into the face. They run into the hundreds,
> in order, and the order has gaps — blocks taken from the middle, which is the
> last place a person takes one from. Below the yard the tailrace comes out of
> the hill.

**Changed:** *a building nobody put up* → *a road nobody laid*.
Everything else is already the new game. Numbered blocks, in order, with gaps
taken from the middle — that is a previous crew's material, stated without
anybody saying so.

## 2 · The Stack — *hard case: this one is pure counting*

**Now**
> Three flat rocks, and under the top one a strip of lead with marks scratched
> into it. Not writing — or not writing you know. The marks are evenly spaced,
> and there are exactly as many of them as there are steps from here back to the
> fork. Somebody counted. Somebody expected to forget.

**Reskinned**
> Three flat rocks, and under the top one an iron stake driven to the collar,
> with a number cut into the head. The number is not the next one. Between here
> and the fork are four more stakes the same, all set, all numbered, none of
> them in the order you would walk them. Somebody laid this line out before
> anyone came up to walk it.

**What you lose:** *"Somebody counted. Somebody expected to forget."* — probably
the best two sentences in the file, and they were written for the AI premise you
just cut.
**What you gain:** the last line does the same job for the new premise, and it
is the premise's whole thesis in one sentence.

---

## 104 · The Counting Room — *hardest case: the machine IS the old theme*

**Now**
> A dry room off the pit, and a machine in it the size of a chair. A cam lifts a
> stylus; the stylus cuts one mark into a lead sheet; the sheet advances by a
> hair. It is counting the wheel. Stacked below it, forty sheets, filled edge to
> edge — and the top one has been started over.

**Reskinned**
> A dry room off the pit, and a plan chest in it the size of a chair. Forty
> sheets, folded in eighths, filed by number. Sections and gradients for ground
> you have not walked, drawn in a hand that is not Rennick's. The top sheet is a
> fair copy of the one below it, and its title block has been left blank.

**What you lose:** the machine, which is a genuinely good image.
**What you gain:** the sheets are the same sheets the post rider brings you.
*A fair copy of the one below it* is the reissue, said once and never explained.
*A hand that is not Rennick's* is the previous crew, said once and never
explained.

# The verdict this produces

**Most of the 37 need nothing, or one word.** The file is full of civil
engineering already: a weir, a haul road worn a hand deep, cut steps, march
stones, cairns sighted one to the next, dressed numbered blocks. Those are not
being reskinned — they are being **read correctly for the first time**.

**About six need real work**, and they are exactly the counting ones — The
Stack, The Tally, The Counting Room, The Chalk Room, The Same Room, The Drum.
They are the load-bearing prose of the theme that was just cut, and they are
good, which is why this is a real loss and not a chore.

**The measured cost:** five or six bodies rewritten and roughly a dozen
single-word swaps. An afternoon, not a week.

⚠️ **One thing to decide with your eyes open.** Two sentences are lost that are
better than anything I will write to replace them:

> *"Somebody counted. Somebody expected to forget."*

They were written for a game about a machine that forgets. That game is cut. The
lines can be kept — they still scan — but they will be pointing at nothing, and
this project's own rule is that prose describing a system the code does not have
is a lie the player eventually catches.
