# 1. WHO YOU ARE

**Third hand on a four-man crew at the end of the last surveyed road.** You are
the one who does the walking. Nobody has told you why the road is being built,
because a crew paid by the span does not need to know.

**Why you are there:** the Crown paid for a road. The crew builds it.

⚠️ **Not an outcast, not a convict.** That premise was developed and rejected:
"sent for an unknown crime" is a mystery that must eventually explain itself at
length, which is the thing the owner is most tired of. *Convict* survives as
texture — your crew is what the frontier gives you — never as the reason.

# 2. THE COUNTRY

The **Marches**: high scrub east of the last tollhouse, granite under a foot of
peat, weather that comes sideways, nobody in it but people who chose it.

A graded road is being cut through it — two carts wide, ditched and metalled,
from the tollhouse to a pass that is on the plan and on no map.

- **The stated reason** is wool. The drove roads flood every spring, and a made
  road can carry a toll.
- **The real reason** is that a road is how a kingdom takes ownership of ground
  it has never governed. The survey stakes are the claim. The road is only the
  argument for the stakes.

# 3. ★ THE GRAPH IS THE PLAN

**This is the single most important line in this document.**

The graph is not a map, not a UI, not a metaphor. It is **the chief engineer's
plan** — a physical drawn sheet the crew carries. Stations inked, joined by
ruled lines. Nodes are surveyed points with numbers cut into them. Edges are
lengths of road not yet built.

Ask a player "what am I looking at" and the answer is a thing that exists in the
fiction: *the plan*. That has never been true in this project before.

And it buys the thing no other premise could:

> **The plan is an object in the world, and it can be wrong.** The ground does
> not match the sheet. Stations are drawn where nothing stands. A line is ruled
> across a moor no cart crosses.

⚠️ **Wrong because it was drawn far away from an old survey** — not wrong
because something uncanny drew it. See §11. There is no twist in this game.

# 4. THE CHIEF ENGINEER

**Chief Engineer Rennick.** Never present. Arrives as sheets folded in eighths,
brought up by the post rider every eight or nine days: alignments, sections,
gradients, a query in the margin, initials. You write back by leaving a chit at
the tollhouse and waiting.

**And he is wrong a lot.** He has never been up here. He is working from a
survey somebody else ran in better weather, and it shows: a culvert specified
for a stream that moved, a gradient that is fine on paper and unwalkable in
peat, a station drawn on ground that turned out to be bog. He is not sinister.
He is a competent man three counties away with an old survey and a budget.

⚠️ **No mystery. Nothing is hidden about him.** He is the reason the plan can be
wrong, and that is his whole job in this game.

# 5. THE FAMILIES

Two or three people each, as asked. Not villages, not vendors.

**Wen and Beatty Hask, charcoal burners.** Two hearths on the north slope,
burning since before the survey. They sell you charcoal at a price they have
plainly thought about, and Beatty writes the tally on a board in a hand that is
much too good. They want the road because charcoal is heavy and the panniers
kill the pony. They think you are honest and temporary, in that order.

**Sal Orme and her boy Pit.** A stove under a sailcloth roof at the second
stake, selling hot food to whoever walks past — which for six years was almost
nobody, and is now you. She wants the road finished slowly and the crews to keep
coming. Pit is eleven and has already learned the whole alignment by heart. Sal
calls you sir and charges you double.

**Tam Ferris and his granddaughter Nell.** Sheep, four hundred of them, and a
drove wall his family did not build. The road crosses his drove at an angle no
sheep will take. He wants a gap left, in stone, at a place he will show you and
no other. He has said this three times. *He does not think you are a bad man; he
thinks you are a man who does what the paper says.*

**The Kessels — Ide, her son Marek, and Marek's wife Dess.** They opened a small
quarry on the promise of the road and are now three people and a hole.
Everything they have is in it. They will work the face themselves for nothing to
have the road reach them before winter. They are the only ones out here who are
glad you came, and they are frightening about it.

⚠️ **A family that does not change a number is a screensaver.** Every one of
them is a supply source, a pair of hands, and one named edge they want built.

# 6. WHAT A NODE IS

Four kinds. The plan is the graph; these are what the plan puts on it.

| kind | what it is | what it is for |
|---|---|---|
| **Stake** | a numbered survey point, not yet anything | small, dim, many. Reaching one reveals what the sheet says about the next — this is how the plan grows |
| **Works** | a length of road being made: a cutting, a culvert, a causeway, a bridge | where the timers live. Most nodes |
| **Holding** | a family's ground | no work of its own. People, goods, favours, and the choices that have people in them |
| **Query** | a place Rennick has ringed in the margin | where trouble is. Rare, and marked |

# 7. ★ THE DECISION THE GAME IS MADE OF

> **Build the plan, or build the spur.**

The plan is an authored optimal subgraph drawn by someone who has never been
here and does not know about the family at the mill. The spur is off it, costs
more, and connects three people who will then feed your crew and shorten your
haul.

**The plan is scored. The spur is not.** That is a choose-your-own-adventure
decision that *moves an edge onto the graph* — the first time in this repo's
history that story and economy have touched the same object.

# 8. THE ECONOMY

**Two stocks. Both are things a road crew actually has.**

⚠️ Two invented currencies have now been rejected in a row — "pace" and
"stone" — and they failed the same way: **a noun chosen for a number, instead of
a number chosen for a noun.** Nothing below is invented.

| stock | what it is | in | out |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Provisions** (loads) | flour, salt pork, fodder, nails — what the wagons carry | hauled from the depot and from connected families, **along built road only** | laying road; planting a depot |
| **Hands** | people in the gang. Start with two | families give them when you serve them | never spent — they set laying speed and larder cap |

The **road** is the third quantity and it is **the graph itself**: not a number,
a picture.

## The one number that reads the topology

```
stock/min = Σ over sources s of  max(0, surplus[s] − HAUL × dist(s, here))

dist  = shortest path in LEAGUES over BUILT road only (∞ if unconnected)
HAUL  = 1 load/min per league
```

**Subtractive, not multiplied.** So every source has a hard **reach radius**
(`surplus / HAUL` leagues), and pushing the face past it means the frontier
stops paying. You have to build supply *behind* you.

This is the only thing a road crew genuinely complains about: the food has to
come from somewhere, and the further out the face is, the more of each wagon the
haulers eat on the way.

**Depots** are the lever. Spend loads at any built node to plant one; it becomes
a source re-radiating whatever reaches it. Planting one is purely a question of
**where**.

## The loop, six lines

1. Tap a node you can reach. The plan shows **leagues, grade, loads**.
2. The crew is **stocking** by default — wagons run, the larder fills, you can
   shut the phone.
3. Larder covers the segment → **Lay**. The gang moves to the face, the larder
   drains, the road goes solid over 30–120s.
4. Road reaches a family → **+1 hand**, surplus, and they name one edge they
   want.
5. The face is now six leagues from supply and stocking has halved. Detour to
   the family, plant a depot, or push on into the deficit.
6. Repeat. An empty larder is never a loss — the gang downs tools and hauls.

## Arithmetic

| | |
|---|---|
| first road | ~70s |
| tenth road | ~18 min cumulative |
| overnight, early (2 hands) | ~12 min banked — larder cap 240 |
| overnight, mid (10 hands) | ~30 min banked → about four segments on return |
| the whole map | **~5 hours** |

Larder cap is `120 × hands` — physical wagon capacity. That is what makes twelve
hours safe without making it enormous.

## Three genre defaults deliberately not done

1. **No upgrade shop.** No multiplier tree, no "+10% haul speed" list. **If it
   is not a place, it is not a lever.**
2. **No tap-to-earn.** No click income, no golden cookie. The gang works whether
   you watch or not. Your only input is *where*.
3. **No unbounded exponentials.** No `1.15^n`, no break_eternity magnitudes.
   Numbers stay in the hundreds all game, because a wagon holds what a wagon
   holds. Cost growth comes from distance and grade — the same seesaw, with a
   cause.

# 9. THE SKILLS

Four, each changing **a different unit**, so no two are the same number wearing
different names.

| skill | trained by | changes | at cap |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Chainage** | surveying a line you have not built | the **stone price** of a road | −46% |
| **Clearing** | clearing what stands on the line | **fill speed** | 1.95× |
| **Grade** | laying road | **what a road carries** | 250 → 725 |
| **Groundwork** | working a family's plot | **what a family makes** | 100 → 290 |

**No skill changes a number in the activity that trains it.** That was the
structural block that kept skills out of this game for weeks; the grid is in the
agent's report and every cell is checked.

Grade and Groundwork both surface as loads/min **on purpose** — and the picture
tells you which you need. Roads drawn saturated → train Grade. Roads drawn slack
→ train Groundwork. **The graph answers the question**, which is the north star.

**Cap 20, each level 40% dearer than the last.** Level 5 is five minutes on that
job; level 10 is 37 minutes; the cap is 18.6 hours. **An overnight cannot max a
skill** — eight hours banked is level 17 from zero — and it can only ever feed
the one skill you left running.

**Never show XP.** Show `next level: 4m 20s` on the job button.

## The tenth level, honestly

Chainage 10 is a road costing 31% less. **That is a slightly smaller number and
nobody will feel it.** The fix: **every fourth level is a step, not a slope.**
Levels 4, 8, 12, 16, 20 are notched, and each is authored — a door opens, a
family arrives, Rennick sends a note, the road draws differently. Between
notches it is a slope, and that is fine *because you can see the next notch and
what is behind it*. Twenty authored beats. Payable.

## Showing a door without a menu

The whole plan is drawn from frame one, so every door is visible from frame one.
Zoomed out, a gated link draws in locked ink — you see *where* the walls are
without reading. Tap it and the panel says **"Grade 9 — you are 6"**. The skill
bar carries a notch at 9 with the place's name under it.

# 10. WHAT REPLACES PRESTIGE

**The survey.** You finish the plan. The kingdom issues a new plan over the same
ground. The roads reset. **What you learned about the terrain does not** — this
ford is passable in summer, this wood eats three days a mile, this family will
board a crew.

Knowledge persists, infrastructure does not, which is how surveying actually
works. It needs no reveal and no reset screen.

# 11. ★ THERE IS NO TWIST

**Decided by the owner, 2026-08-02:** *"just your typical low fantasy with no
twist."*

An earlier draft of this document had one — the plan turning out to be a reissue
of an older survey, Rennick reissuing rather than designing, the drove wall being
the last road gone back to grass. **It is cut.** So is the AI reveal it was
quietly standing in for. `docs/attic/` has both.

⚠️ **This is a real constraint on everything written from here, not a note.**
Every time a piece of prose in this project has been good, somebody has tried to
make it *mean* something, and the meaning has always been the same shape: it was
all secretly about a machine. That is finished.

**What carries the game instead — and this is enough:**

- **The work.** It is hard, it is slow, and the ground fights you. That is the
  whole of Prince Avalanche and most of Firewatch.
- **The people.** Tam Ferris wants a gap in a wall. The Kessels have everything
  they own in a hole and need you to reach them before winter. Sal Orme wants
  the road to take a long time. **These want incompatible things and you cannot
  satisfy all of them** — that is the drama, and it needs no reveal to work.
- **The plan being wrong.** Not sinister: just drawn far away, from an old
  survey, by a man with a budget. You are the one standing in the bog he ruled a
  line across.

**The frontier has history and no secret.** There was a road here before, in
places — old walls, a drove, cut stone in the wrong spot. That is what an old
country looks like. **Nobody is going to explain it, because there is nothing to
explain.**

# 12. TONE

| touchstone | what we take |
|---|---|
| **Prince Avalanche** — almost certainly the asphalt film | two men doing repetitive work in emptied country; the drama is small and between people |
| **Firewatch** | a task, a radius, and the sense that someone set this up before you got here |
| **The Straight Story** | slowness is the point, not the obstacle |
| **Alan Garner** | landscape that keeps count — the register the existing place prose already writes in |
| **Early RuneScape** | a shut door is a short wait; numbers you can see from here |

**Nothing rhymes. Nothing is wise. The prose observes and stops.**

# ★ WHAT THE OWNER STILL HAS TO DECIDE

The five agents genuinely disagreed on these. Nothing gets built until they are

## A. The 37 hand-written places — keep or reskin?

They are already civil engineering: a weir, a haul road worn a hand deep, a
blockyard of dressed numbered blocks *"waiting for a building nobody put up"*,
cut steps, march stones, cairns sighted one to the next.

- **KEEP near-verbatim.** They were never about a valley — they are about
  somebody who built infrastructure for no visible purpose and kept a tally.
  Under this premise that becomes *a previous crew ran this plan*, which is the
  slow realisation for free. Cost: a frame and a numbering pass.
- **RESKIN, and bin the counting spine.** The bodies fit, but the tally, the
  lead strip and the Counting Room are the old ontology theme wearing a hat.
  Cost: about a day of prose, five or six places rewritten.

Worked reskin of **The Stack**, so the trade is visible:

> *Three flat rocks, and under the top one an iron stake driven to the collar,
> with a number cut into the head. The number is not the next one. Between here
> and the fork are four more stakes the same, all set, all numbered, none of
> them in the order you would walk them. Somebody laid this line out before
> anyone came up to walk it.*

You lose *"somebody expected to forget."* You gain the plan already being here.

## ~~B. What replaces combat?~~ ✅ DECIDED 2026-08-02: **obstacles with a cost**

Something holds the line — a washout, a rockfall, a landowner who says no.
Clearing it costs time and loads and trains Clearing. The duel that is currently
built (`holder`, `might`, `bite`, radius-is-health) is **retired**: a road crew
stabbing things is the old game leaking into the new one. `BRIEF.md` ask 7 is
therefore reinterpreted, not deleted — a thing stands in your way and you remove
it, but it is work rather than a fight.

## ~~C. Which economy?~~ ✅ DECIDED 2026-08-02: **both, haulage first**

Haulage is built first and must be fun on its own: supply has to reach the face,
distance subtracts, every source has a reach radius, depots are the lever. Then
throughput layers on top — a road also has a capacity, and a saturated one needs
a second beside it. They compose, and nothing built for the first is thrown away
by the second.

⚠️ **Haulage first means the shipped max-flow becomes a shortest-path** for a
while. `flow.ts` keeps its shape; the solve changes.

<details><summary>the options as they were put</summary>

Two models arrived and they are not the same game.

- **Haulage** (§8): distance from supply subtracts from what arrives. Reach
  radius. Build supply behind you.
- **Throughput** (what is shipped today): max-flow, roads have a capacity, a
  saturated road needs a second one beside it.

They can be combined, but the first thing built should be one of them.
